# Business

Identify the decision owner, objective, alternatives, constraints, and time
horizon. Separate observed results from forecasts and stated assumptions.
One successful company case does not establish a universal strategy.

## Ways to show the idea

Use [Excalidraw](../../excalidraw/SKILL.md) for fixed process and responsibility
maps, [Pinepaper](../../pinepaper/SKILL.md) for linked process states or
scenario graphs, and tables for exact choices and cash. Use
[Manim](../../manim-voice-animation/SKILL.md) when work or information moving
between stages explains a delay or failure. Keep case facts separate from
illustrative numbers; a polished map does not establish actual behavior.

Business owns the operating decision. Accounting checks recognition, economics
supports the market mechanism, and psychology supports behavioral claims.
No business teacher is assigned by default.

## Teaching each area

Choose the matching section. Its order suggests how to build the topic; it is
not a complete syllabus. Course design uses the starting point and source
checks. Lesson design chooses the views that explain the difficult steps.
Treat the named confusion as a possibility, not a diagnosis of this learner.

### Strategy

- **Build:** Start with a customer need and two feasible choices under real constraints.
  Compare expanding an existing offer with entering a new segment. Separate
  an attractive market from the firm's ability to serve it profitably.
- **Research:** Use a dated case with filings or operating evidence and a contrasting
  case. Check which explanation is supported beyond the successful outcome.
- **Show and check:** Put both choices, constraints, and supporting facts in a
  decision table. Trace the claimed advantage through the activities that
  produce it in a value-chain map; connect each dependency to its cost or
  customer consequence in the table. Remove one capability or change a
  competitor's response and ask whether the recommendation still follows.
  Case evidence supports the explanation; the map alone does not.

### Operations

- **Build:** Start with one item moving through a process. Improving a busy station need
  not improve total throughput. Use a small order passing through preparation,
  processing, and delivery; distinguish waiting time from service time.
- **Research:** Use operations research for the process model and actual process
  observations for inputs. Check service times, variation, and capacity assumptions.
- **Show and check:** Label each stage in the process map with the service
  time and resources used in the capacity table. Follow one order's elapsed
  time, then compare it with the rate of completed orders. A queue trace must
  use the same arrivals and service assumptions. Speed up a non-bottleneck
  stage, then the bottleneck, and ask which improvement changes throughput
  and which may change delay.

### Marketing

- **Build:** Start with the hypothesis that a message helps a stated customer
  group buy. Clicks, purchase, and retention are separate outcomes; more clicks
  need not mean more customers or a causal effect of the message.
- **Research:** Inspect customer research and the experiment or analytics definitions.
  Check attribution, cohort selection, measurement period, and the outcome that matters.
- **Show and check:** Build a funnel from the cohort table, preserving stage
  counts, denominators, and observation windows. An experiment diagram shows
  how customers entered each message condition; relate its outcome to the
  corresponding funnel stage. Change the cohort or denominator and ask
  whether the apparent improvement survives. Use the design and uncertainty
  to judge the claim, not the funnel's shape.

### Organizations

- **Build:** Start with a shared task and who has information, authority, and
  incentives. Follow a missed customer escalation; distinguish a person's
  action from the role conflict or missing information that shaped it.
- **Research:** Use an organizational study and records of the actual workflow. Compare
  formal roles with observed authority, incentives, and communication.
- **Show and check:** Put the escalation's dated handoffs on a timeline and
  connect each to the responsible role in a map. Mark authority separately
  from information flow so an arrow does not imply both. Use records to
  distinguish the formal procedure from the observed path. Give a role new
  authority but the same information, then ask which failure remains.

### Finance and entrepreneurship

- **Build:** Start with a sale, its costs, and when cash arrives or leaves. Profit,
  cash, and available runway differ. An unpaid invoice can support accrual
  profit while leaving the business unable to meet an earlier cash payment.
- **Research:** Use company filings or a clearly labeled teaching case with finance
  definitions. Check cash timing, financing terms, and sensitivity to operating
  assumptions.
- **Show and check:** Use one sale in the unit-economics table and cash-flow
  timeline, distinguishing recognition from collection. Build each period's
  cash balance from opening cash and dated receipts and payments; trace the
  low point back to its transactions. Extend the payment delay and ask whether
  margin, cash needs, or both change. Any runway graph must use the same
  financing and operating assumptions.

## Source use

Use [the source-use guide](../references/source-use.md). Catalog entries are
