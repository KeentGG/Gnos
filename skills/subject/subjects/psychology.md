# Psychology

Identify the construct, its measure, the population, and the comparison.
Distinguish reliability from validity, a sample from its population, and
association from causation. Do not infer a stable trait or diagnosis from one
response.

## Ways to show the idea

Use experiment diagrams, timelines, and measured distributions. Use
[Excalidraw](../../excalidraw/SKILL.md) for fixed study structure,
[Pinepaper](../../pinepaper/SKILL.md) for timed stimulus scenes,
[JSXGraph](../../jsxgraph/SKILL.md) for quantitative model comparisons,
and [Manim](../../manim-voice-animation/SKILL.md) when timing
needs narration. Audio or images may be the stimulus itself. A simulation can
show sampling, measurement error, or confounding under stated assumptions.
Connect the displayed result to what was actually measured.

Psychology owns behavioral interpretation; biology supports a mechanism and
math supports inference. No psychology teacher is assigned by default.

## Teaching each area

Choose the matching section. Its order suggests how to build the topic; it is
not a complete syllabus. Course design uses the starting point and source
checks. Lesson design chooses the views that explain the difficult steps.
Treat the named confusion as a possibility, not a diagnosis of this learner.

### Cognition and learning

- **Build:** Start with remembering a short list after a delay, or another
  task suited to the concept. Define the measured response. Better performance
  on that task need not establish a general capacity or explain its mechanism.
- **Research:** Use an academic chapter for the proposed mechanism and the original task
  protocol for the finding. Inspect timing, scoring, comparison, and replication
  evidence.
- **Show and check:** Put stimulus, delay, and response on a protocol timeline,
  then connect scored responses to table rows and a distribution. A study
  diagram shows which comparison tests the proposed mechanism. Keep an
  interactive demonstration separate from measured study results. Change
  delay or scoring and ask what each explanation predicts and whether the
  original measure still captures the intended ability.

### Development

- **Build:** Start with scores observed at two ages. Ask whether they belong
  to the same people or different groups before describing development;
  age, cohort, and period can produce different explanations.
- **Research:** Inspect a developmental study with its sampling and follow-up design.
  Check age, cohort, attrition, and whether comparisons follow the same people.
- **Show and check:** Place birth cohorts and measurement dates in a table
  and timeline, showing which observations follow the same participants.
  Derive trajectories from those rows, preserving missing follow-ups rather
  than inventing continuous change. Contrast a between-age difference with
  a within-person change. Remove one follow-up group or change the observation
  period and ask how the developmental interpretation is limited.

### Social and personality

- **Build:** Start with the same person responding in two situations, such as
  a private and public choice. A response does not establish a stable trait;
  a difference does not isolate the situation's effect without a sound comparison.
- **Research:** Inspect the measure, study design, and replication or review evidence.
  Check situation, population, effect size, and variation within groups.
- **Show and check:** Keep person and situation labels in a response table,
  then map its within-person and between-person comparisons to the study
  diagram. Sourced distributions show group overlap that a mean alone hides.
  Introduce repeated observations or a different situation and ask which
  trait or situational explanation remains plausible. Preserve effect size,
  population, and uncertainty before generalizing.

### Biological psychology

- **Build:** Start with a measured response during a task and a proposed
  biological mechanism. A neural signal is a measurement with limits, not
  a direct reading of a thought or proof that a region causes the behavior.
- **Research:** Use a neuroscience reference for the mechanism and the original
  measurement study for the claim. Check spatial and temporal resolution and alternative
  interpretations.
- **Show and check:** Locate the measured structure in an anatomy diagram
  and align task events with the signal trace at the instrument's actual
  resolution. Connect the study figure's contrast to those conditions; explain
  each transformation from recorded signal to displayed summary. Change a
  task contrast or propose an alternative physiological source and ask what
  the signal can distinguish. Label schematic anatomy and assumed pathways.

### Clinical psychology

- **Build:** Start with a clearly labeled educational case and a study's
  population and question. Similar symptoms can have different contexts and
  courses; they do not establish an individual's diagnosis or treatment.
- **Research:** Use current professional guidance and systematic reviews for educational
  claims. Check population, diagnostic criteria, study design, outcomes, and limits of
  applicability.
- **Show and check:** Use a dated criteria excerpt and case timeline to
  explain duration, context, and missing information. For a treatment claim,
  connect eligibility and comparison groups in the study diagram to the
  outcomes and uncertainty in the plot. Change the case's duration or the
  study population and ask what can still be inferred. Do not turn group
  results or a teaching case into a personal clinical conclusion.

### Research methods

- **Build:** Start with a construct such as attention and a task score meant
  to measure it. Consistent scores can reflect a different ability; an
  association between scores can also reflect a common cause.
- **Research:** Use a methods text plus the study protocol and analysis. Inspect
  operational definitions, reliability, validity, uncertainty, and replication.
- **Show and check:** Trace construct, task, response, and scoring in a
  measurement table; map the same scores to a distribution with units and
  sample stated. A causal diagram marks assumed alternatives, not proven
  paths. Compare a reliable but poorly matched measure with a better-targeted
  one, or change a simulated confound, and ask what claim each permits.
  Inspect actual protocol, uncertainty, and replication evidence separately
  from any toy model.
